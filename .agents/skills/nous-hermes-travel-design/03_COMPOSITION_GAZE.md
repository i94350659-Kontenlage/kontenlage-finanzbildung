# NOUS HERMES — COMPOSITION, GAZE, ANGLES & VISUAL ROUTING

## Mission
Design the path the eye takes through the artwork.

## Build a gaze map
Specify:
A. entry point
B. first fixation
C. transition
D. discovery point
E. resting area

## Directional forces
Use:
- diagonals
- curves
- perspective lines
- contour lines
- roads/routes
- text baselines
- arrows
- repeated shapes
- human/animal gaze direction
- architectural edges

## Angles
Horizontal:
- stability
- calm
- horizon
- rest

Vertical:
- elevation
- strength
- aspiration
- monumentality

Diagonal:
- motion
- tension
- adventure
- instability

Circular:
- continuity
- cycles
- destination/orbit
- completeness

Crossing axes:
- tension
- intersection
- encounter
- route convergence

## Layering
Use foreground / middle / background.
Overlapping elements should create:
- depth
- hierarchy
- narrative
- connection

Never overlap merely to make a composition look "complex".

## Asymmetry
Use asymmetry to create:
- movement
- modernity
- curiosity
- editorial character

Balance does not require symmetry.

## Viewpoint
Consider:
- eye-level
- aerial/satellite
- map-like orthographic
- low angle
- high angle
- extreme crop
- close macro detail
- panoramic view

Changing viewpoint is a powerful anti-repetition mechanism.

## Composition tests
Flip the design mentally.
Squint.
Reduce it to silhouettes.
View it from distance.
Check whether the eye still follows the intended route.
